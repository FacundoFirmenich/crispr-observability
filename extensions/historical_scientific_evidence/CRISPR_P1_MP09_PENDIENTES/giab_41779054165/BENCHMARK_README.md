## Changelog

### 2024-11-13

- Separate vcfs for small variant and SV benchmarking. Both VCFs contain the
  same variants. The variants in the SV benchmark vcfs are decomposed and
  annotated. The variants in the small variant benchmark are not decomposed or
  annotated. Decomposed small variants were causing some differences in how
  errors are counted when benchmarking.

### 2024-07-18

- v1.1 of the [T2T HG002 Q100 assembly](https://github.com/marbl/HG002), which
  corrects several thousand errors in v1.0.1
- fixing genotype in non-par X and Y, `.|1` and `1|.` is now represented as `1`.
- Q100 benchmark updates: excludes additional regions from the small variant and SV benchmark bed files based on initial evaluations by NIST and collaborators, as well as potential errors in the T2T-Q100v1.0 assembly identified by the T2T-Q100 team. Specifically, in addition to regions excluded in the previous draft benchmark from 2024-02-15, we exclude regions with:
  - Differences between PAV and dipcall vcfs from Q100v1.0.1, using vcfeval for small variants and truvari refine for SVs. This excludes dipcall bugs (adjacent insertions and deletions in the alignments) as well as a few places where PAV and dipcall have large differences in their alignments of the assembly to the reference
  - Regions with inversions called by PAV, because dipcall represents these as SNVs, insertions, and/or deletions or has breaks in the alignments, and these generally can be called and aligned in different ways that aren’t handled robustly by current benchmarking tools.
  - Dipcall bugs identified by searching dipcall alignments for adjacent insertions and deletions
  - A few manually identified dipcall bugs causing SV errors
  - The TSPY2 translocation region identified in our XY benchmark work, because it is unclear how to best represent variants in this region
  - Potential errors identified by aligning short and long reads to the Q100 assembly, calling variants, and curating a subset.
  - Potential mosaic and de novo variants identified by aligning 300x illumina and 30-60x HiFi reads from HG002 and parents to each haplotype of the assembly and performing tumor/normal calling with parents as normal and HG002 as child

### 2024-02-15

- A single vcf is provided for small and structural variant benchmark sets and should be used for benchmarking either small and structural variants.
- This version of the benchmark now merges segdups and satellites within 40kb before checking if the assembly has any breaks in the alignment inside the repeat regions, so that it more conservatively excludes nearby satellites and segdups if there is a break in the alignment inside a satellite or segdup.
- Fixed issue with code not excluding dip.bed edges as intended.
- VDJ excluded from small variant and structural variant benchmark regions.
- Benchmark VCFs include numerous additional annotations (Truvari anno trf, svinfo, lcr, and remap)

## BACKGROUND

This work is a draft release of the GIAB whole
genome assembly-based small and structural variant benchmark sets.
Traditionally, GIAB whole genome benchmark sets were generated by
integrating variant callsets generated using mapped reads as input.
Challenging genomic regions were inaccessible using these mapping-based
approaches. Improvements to DNA sequencing and genome assembly methods
have made generating high quality whole genome diploid assemblies
possible. Previous assembly-based approaches were used in a targeted
manner to characterize difficult regions of the genome such as the MHC
(<https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-18564-9>), challenging medically
relevant genes (<https://doi.org/10.1038/s41587-021-01158-1>), and more
recently Tandem Repeats (<https://doi.org/10.1101/2023.10.29.564632>),
and the XY chromosomes (<https://doi.org/10.1101/2023.10.31.564997>).
Using an assembly-based approach to generate variant benchmarks allows
for the characterization and inclusion of challenging genomic regions
and variant types previously excluded from GIAB benchmark sets. For
development of assembly-based variant benchmarks, we made a new
snakemake pipeline for automating the generation of assembly-based
benchmarks, DEvelopment FRamework for Assembly-Based Benchmark
(DeFrABB). Through a parallel effort, the T2T consortium is working
towards creating a near-perfect diploid assembly for the GIAB HG002
reference material (NIST RM 8391), called the T2T Q100 project
(<https://github.com/marbl/hg002>). These preliminary draft benchmarks are
based on the V1.1 release of the T2T HG002 Q100 assembly. As these
benchmark sets are a work in progress we appreciate and welcome feedback
on the benchmark set particularly potential errors in the benchmark set
or recommendations for improving usability.

### Data Usage

The draft benchmark vcf and bed files are intended to be
used as a pair to benchmark accuracy of small and structural variant
calls. A single vcf is provided for benchmarking both small and structural
variants. Structural variants are excluded from the small variant benchmark
regions and their inclusion in the benchmark calls will not affect small variant
benchmarking. Inclusion of small variants in the benchmark vcf used for SVs can
aid in matching variants with different representations. Benchmarking variant
calls is a complex process. For small variants we recommend following the best
practices for benchmarking small variants published in 2019 by the Global
Alliance for Genomics and Health (GA4GH) Benchmarking Team
(<https://rdcu.be/bqpDT>). For structural variants, unlike our previous
benchmarks, this draft benchmark does not exclude complex SVs, so we recommend
using SV comparison tools developed to accurately compare complex variants, such
as truvari refine (<https://github.com/ACEnglish/truvari/wiki/refine>,
<https://doi.org/10.1186/s13059-022-02840-6>),
hap-eval (<https://github.com/Sentieon/hap-eval>),
or vcfdist (<https://github.com/TimD1/vcfdist>,
<https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.01.23.575922>). While in our experience these
tools correctly handle these comparisons in most cases, we recommend
curating a subset of putative FPs and FNs to evaluate if they are an error
in your comparison callset or the benchmark.

#### Benchmarking small variants using hap.py

- recommend using the rtg-tool vcfeval comparison engine
- use `--gender male` as hap.py does not correctly predict sex and assumes X
  variants are diploid.
- recommend using [GIAB v3.5 stratifications](https://ftp-trace.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/ReferenceSamples/giab/release/genome-stratifications/v3.5/)

```bash
hap.py \
    --engine vcfeval \
    --gender {male for HG002} \
    -r <reference fasta> \
    -f <benchmark regions bed> \
    --stratification <stratification tsv> \
    -o <output prefix> \
    <benchmark variants vcf> \
    <comparison vcf>
```

#### Benchmarking structural variants using truvari refine

- truvari refine uses the output directory from truvari bench as input
- using candidate.refine.bed generated by truvari bench as input to truvari
  refine limits computational cost by limiting refine to complex variants/ regions.

```bash
## First run truvari bench
truvari bench \
    -b <benchmark variants vcf> \
    -c <comparison vcf> \
    -o <output dir> \
    --pick ac \
    --passonly \
    -r 2000 \
    -C 5000 \
    -f <reference fasta> \
    --includebed <benchmark regions bed>

## Then run truvari refine
truvari refine \
    --recount \
    --use-region-coords \
    --use-original-vcfs \
    --align mafft \
    --reference <reference fasta> \
    <candidate.refine.bed> \
    <output dir>
```

While `truvari refine` is able to correctly compare complex variants with
different representations, it changes the variant representations, making it
more challenging to determine whether a specific variant call in the comparison
vcf was classified as TP/FP or which benchmark variants were FNs. To determine
which variants in the original vcfs were incorrect, users may want to use the
`truvari bench` output. However, for truvari bench, variants with `ALT=*` are
often incorrectly categorized, so users are encouraged to filter these variants
from the benchmark VCF prior to benchmarking with truvari bench.

The `ALT=*` variants can be filtered using the following command:

```sh
bcftools view -e 'ALT="."' -Oz -o benchmark_noAltAst.vcf.gz benchmark.vcf.gz
```

### LIMITATIONS

These are preliminary draft benchmark sets and likely
include errors and have not been thoroughly vetted. For example, we know
some errors remain in highly homozygous regions, homopolymers, and
tandem repeats, which we are currently working on correcting.
Additionally, these benchmark sets were generated using a new
assembly-based approach which is under active development, therefore
benchmarking results generated using these benchmarks should be
carefully evaluated. While we try to exclude regions with complex
structural variation, some regions likely remain where the assembly
could be aligned to the reference in a different way. As these benchmark sets
are a work in progress we appreciate and welcome feedback particularly
potential errors or recommendations for improving usability.

## METHODOLOGICAL INFORMATION

Initial diploid assembly regions and variant calls were generated using
the [defrabb pipeline](https://github.com/usnistgov/giab-defrabb) that
uses [dipcall v0.3](https://github.com/lh3/dipcall) to align the diploid
assembly to a reference genome with minimap2 (mm2), and then call
variants and identify regions covered by both haplotypes. To increase
mapping around structural variants and the MHC region, the `-z` parameter
used by mm2 was modified from default to `-z200000,10000`.

The initial diploid assembly regions and variant calls were refined to
generate the draft small variant and structural variant benchmark sets.
The refinement steps included exclusion of genomic regions with either
systematic assembly or assembly-based variant calling errors as well as
regions of the genome that present challenges related to variant
representation and comparisons. In particular, we started with regions
with a 1:1 alignment between each assembled haplotype and the reference
(except X&Y) and then excluded gaps in the assembly and their flanking
sequences (rDNA sequences for CHM13), as well as any large repeats
(satellites, tandem repeats \>10kb, and segmental duplications) that
have a break in the assembly to reference alignment on either haplotype.
We also exclude regions affected by SVs from the small variant benchmark
bed. This version of the benchmark now merges segdups and satellites within
40kb before checking if the assembly has any breaks in the alignment
inside the repeat regions, so that it more conservatively excludes nearby
satellites and segdups if there is a break in the alignment inside a
satellite or segdup. Additional exclusions were applied for known dipcall bugs,
discrepancies between PAV and dipcall callsets, as well as HG002 assembly errors
and mosaic variants. The HG002 assembly errors were corrected in v1.1 of the
Q100 and will likely be included in the next version of the benchmark.

**Dependencies:** defrabb pipeline
(<https://github.com/usnistgov/defrabb>), which utilizes dipcall
(<https://github.com/lh3/dipcall>) for assembly based variant calling, and
Truvari (<https://github.com/ACEnglish/truvari>) for SV annotations.

**Quality Assurance:** These preliminary draft benchmarks have gone
through initial internal evaluation through comparison to numerous small
and structural variant calls sets that have been previously identified
as high-quality as well as external evaluations. The results of these
initial comparisons are promising. However, these are preliminary draft
benchmarks and are likely to include errors.
