# A practical observability audit

1. **Name the target.** Distinguish software modification, exact programmed DNA,
   local peptide and genotype. A matched label requires a matched question.
2. **Keep the recorded universe.** Report E, NE, U and N with exact endpoints;
   retain native caller percentages under their native denominators.
3. **Test product-dependent containment.** Record the required interval, read end,
   displacement, strand and quality mask. Geometric containment does not imply
   unique motifs or capture before sequencing.
4. **Check correspondence.** Compare observed strings, coordinates and insertion
   ownership. Preserve mismatches between labels and strings.
5. **Name the candidate domain.** A certificate needs a nonempty complete domain
   and an invariant determinate target. Two agreeing selected projections do not
   establish completeness. Path sampling cannot certify that no opposite path exists.
6. **Measure local withdrawal costs.** Preserve discordant calls removed,
   concordant calls lost and unknowns per locus/library/event. Fix admission rules
   before scoring where the design permits and record actual decision timing.
7. **Export the operation and its evidence.** Save input identities, effective
   parameters, outputs, environment and code revision. Use the graph to inspect
   support and the laboratory to reproduce a specific parameter intervention.

The fixed raw denominator covers acquired records. PCR/library selection can
remove molecules before that universe exists. Original-fragment biological
accuracy therefore requires a suitable independent observation and authenticated
linkage; a replay or a 3D rendering cannot supply it.
